# Adaptive Strategy Interview Playbook

Use this playbook when the user asks to discuss strategy, provides an incomplete product list, or requests an interview before a strategy document.

## Interview rules

1. Ask one question at a time.
2. Ask no more than four questions in one collection round, and only when the interaction format supports a batch.
3. Start from the decision with the highest downside or greatest ability to change priorities.
4. Explain briefly why a sensitive number or legal fact matters.
5. Offer a reasonable working assumption only when it is low-risk and clearly labeled.
6. Summarize confirmed decisions after each coherent block; do not repeatedly ask what is already known.
7. Challenge contradictions with evidence and neutral language.
8. Stop discovery when enough information exists to make the requested decision. Do not turn interviewing into a ritual.

## Question selection algorithm

At each turn:

1. List unknowns that could change the decision.
2. Rank them by impact on cash survival, legal feasibility, delivery capacity, customer priority, and irreversibility.
3. Ask the highest-ranked question that the user can answer.
4. Update the evidence ledger and choose the next question.

Prefer a concrete question such as “Đơn vị giữ lại bao nhiêu trên mỗi học viên sau khi chia cho kênh tuyển sinh, và số đó đã trừ lương bộ máy chưa?” over “Chi phí của anh thế nào?”.

## Discovery sequence

Skip any block already answered.

### A. Decision and ambition

- What decision must this strategy enable?
- What does success look like at 12 months and 3 years?
- Which legal entity or business unit is in scope?
- What is explicitly out of scope?
- Who will read the formal artifact, and what titles/names must it use?

### A2. Ecosystem center and boundaries

- Which component owns trust, the input funnel, community, and the recurring relationship?
- Is the institution the ecosystem center, a supporting education node, or both in different business lines?
- Which programs reinforce the core ecosystem, and which are relatively independent education businesses?
- Who owns revenue, liability, learner data, outcome data, and post-program support in each path?

### B. Priority customer

- Who must be served first, second, and third?
- What urgent job or risk makes each segment buy now?
- Who decides and who pays?
- Is the buyer an individual, business owner, employer, parent, partner, or government-funded party?

### C. Current cash engine

- Which product currently pays the bills?
- What are paid volume, price, retained revenue, contribution, fixed cost, and monthly operating result?
- What is the payment schedule and receivable/refund exposure?
- What runway or survival threshold constrains the plan?
- Which products must self-fund the operating structure, and which are intended to generate future profit or growth?
- Can each economic engine be reported separately before shared costs are allocated?

Resolve definitions explicitly: billings, revenue, retained revenue, gross profit, contribution, operating profit, and cash.

When a user supplies target endpoints but not intermediate months, ask whether to build a planning curve. Label any interpolation as an estimate, not a forecast.

### D. Distribution

- What percentage comes from direct, B2B, community, school, employer, or other channels?
- How concentrated is the channel among top partners?
- Who owns the lead, sale, learner relationship, payment, and data?
- What commercial terms cannot be changed without hurting volume?
- What is the complete price waterfall across institution, network builder, agent, internal sales, promotions, and support?
- Does “direct” enrollment still carry internal commission, sales-support, and processing cost?
- Which document-completion and learner-care tasks can a B2B partner own, under what SLA and data controls?

### E. Delivery and capacity

- What is the current monthly or cohort capacity, and which step is the bottleneck?
- Which tasks are manual, delayed, error-prone, or dependent on one person?
- Who owns admission, academics, assessment, credentialing, learner care, and technology?
- What can be delivered today without new hiring or unproven partners?

### F. Legal and academic basis

- What programs and modalities are currently permitted?
- Who recruits, contracts, collects tuition, teaches, assesses, owns records, and issues credentials?
- Which claims or documents require formal legal/regulatory verification?
- What original documents or human approvals remain mandatory even after digitization?

### G. Portfolio and progression

- Which offers are short, formal, progression, employment, startup, or strategic-option products?
- What learner pathway connects them?
- Which launches have fixed dates and which are only ideas?
- What product must be deferred to protect the core?

### H. Partners

- What is each partner's status: idea, conversation, verbal commitment, MOU, proposal, contract, pilot, or live delivery?
- What will the partner contribute: curriculum, faculty, facilities, equipment, recruitment, projects, internships, jobs, technology?
- How will revenue, cost, data, brand, learner support, liability, and termination work?
- What evidence must the partner provide before launch?

### I. Outcomes

- What outcome is promised and within what measurement window?
- How will completion, employment, business launch, progression, or overseas placement be verified?
- Who owns outcome data after graduation?
- What is the target denominator: graduates, enrolled learners, or respondents?

### J. Implementation readiness

- Who is Accountable and who is Responsible for each workstream?
- How much capacity is actually available in the next phase?
- What source of truth, SOP, data, tools, and approvals already exist?
- What evidence defines ready-to-pilot and ready-to-scale?
- Does each department have a digital workflow, shared data, an AI/human task boundary, adoption evidence, and a management dashboard?
- Is the user asking for an executive phase plan or a detailed cutover checklist? Do not default to daily tasks.

### K. Artifact and communication standard

- Should the deliverable be a discussion checkpoint, strategy brief, DOCX, PDF, slide deck, or implementation register?
- Which terms should remain in English, and which should be written in plain Vietnamese?
- Which relationships require a real diagram rather than prose or text arrows?
- What formal title map must be applied? For Phoenix strategy artifacts, use **Thầy Khiêm**, not **Anh Khiêm**.

## Contradiction handling

When values conflict:

1. Repeat both values without choosing one.
2. Ask whether they differ by time period, product, channel, definition, or actual versus target.
3. Record the reconciled definition.
4. Do not use the disputed number in calculations until resolved.

Example:

`Tài liệu vận hành và số liệu trao đổi đang dùng hai mức sản lượng khác nhau. Hai số có thể khác nhau giữa “hồ sơ tuyển sinh” và “học viên thực học”, hoặc khác kỳ báo cáo. Anh xác nhận giúp định nghĩa và kỳ nào dùng để tính công suất hiện tại?`

## Periodic synthesis

After a major block, summarize:

- strategic thesis now confirmed;
- priority segments;
- economics definitions;
- constraints and risks;
- decisions made;
- the next unresolved decision.

Invite correction only where it can change the strategy.
