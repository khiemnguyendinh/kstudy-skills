# BCG Model for Education Product Portfolios

Use this reference to decide where an education business should defend, nurture, scale, harvest, reduce, reposition, partner, or exit. Use BCG as a portfolio allocation lens, not as a substitute for legal, academic, financial, delivery, or learner-outcome judgment.

## 1. Position the model

BCG answers two linked questions:

1. Is the relevant market or demand pool growing?
2. Does this product have a strong competitive position in that market?

Use the result to allocate scarce management attention, owner capacity, delivery capacity, and investment. Do not use BCG to:

- replace product P&L or cash-flow analysis;
- override a fatal legal, academic, quality, or learner-protection gate;
- classify products from leadership opinion alone;
- prescribe universal budget percentages;
- force every product into a quadrant when evidence is insufficient.

Classify the evidence as **Đã xác nhận**, **Giả định cần kiểm chứng**, **Ước tính kế hoạch**, or **Khuyến nghị**.

## 2. Define the strategic unit

Do not classify a broad course name when performance differs materially by customer or delivery context. Define the narrowest decision unit supported by data:

`product × segment × channel × delivery model × geography × period`

Examples of different units include the same AI course sold B2C through webinars, sold B2B to companies, or delivered through a school partner. Aggregate only when the demand, economics, delivery, and competitive position are genuinely comparable.

For every unit, state:

- learner/customer and buyer;
- job-to-be-done and promised outcome;
- credential or legal basis;
- acquisition channel and revenue owner;
- delivery and outcome owner;
- time window and cohort;
- role in the portfolio: entry, cash engine, core transformation, progression, outcome service, or strategic option.

## 3. Pass prerequisite gates

Run BCG after checking the fatal gates in [quality-gates.md](quality-gates.md). At minimum verify:

- legal and academic feasibility;
- retained revenue, contribution, cash timing, and stop-loss;
- curriculum, faculty, assessment, learner support, and bottleneck capacity;
- outcome quality and claim evidence;
- partner and dependency readiness where relevant.

Record a failed fatal gate separately from the BCG quadrant. A product may appear attractive on the matrix but still require **hold**, **pilot only**, or **no-go**.

## 4. Define the two axes

### Axis A: Market growth or demand attractiveness

Prefer external market growth for the specific segment and need. If reliable market data is unavailable, use an explicit proxy set and label the result **BCG điều chỉnh**.

Possible evidence:

- growth of qualified demand, applications, or paid enrollments in the relevant segment;
- employer demand or partner pipeline with documented conversion;
- search, inquiry, or event demand tied to the same buyer and outcome;
- category growth from a named, dated, credible source;
- willingness to pay and price resilience;
- regulatory, technology, or labor-market change that creates verified demand.

Do not use total institutional revenue growth as a market-growth proxy without separating price, promotion, channel expansion, seasonality, and one-off cohorts.

Define:

- metric and formula;
- data source and owner;
- comparison period, normally enough to expose seasonality;
- threshold for high/low growth;
- confidence: high, medium, or low.

Set thresholds from verified market benchmarks, agreed strategic targets, or the portfolio distribution. If none exists, show **chưa đủ dữ liệu** instead of inventing a universal cutoff.

### Axis B: Relative competitive strength

For classical BCG, use:

`relative market share = product market share ÷ market share of the largest competitor`

When trustworthy share data is unavailable, use an agreed proxy score and label it **BCG điều chỉnh**. Possible evidence:

- conversion and win rate against named alternatives;
- channel ownership and cost to reach the buyer;
- brand trust or referral strength in the target segment;
- differentiated curriculum, credential, experts, data, community, or partner access;
- learner completion, assessment, outcome, renewal, and referral evidence;
- contribution and delivery efficiency compared with credible internal or external benchmarks;
- replacement difficulty and dependency risk.

Do not hide weak outcome quality or poor economics inside a high composite score. Keep those as visible gates or overlays.

Define the benchmark, weights, formula, source, period, and threshold before scoring. Show unknown criteria as unknown.

## 5. Classify the four groups

| Group | Evidence pattern | Management intent | Default actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Star** | High market growth and strong relative position | Scale while protecting quality, outcomes, cash, and delivery capacity | Fund bottlenecks, strengthen channel and differentiation, monitor cohort quality, set scale gates |
| **Tiềm năng cần nuôi dưỡng (Question Mark)** | High market growth and weak or unproven position | Buy evidence cheaply and decide whether to build advantage | Narrow pilot, cap investment, choose one segment/channel, define proof and deadline, partner when faster |
| **Phòng thủ/dòng tiền (Cash Cow)** | Low market growth and strong relative position | Protect dependable cash and trust while increasing efficiency | Defend profitable segments, simplify variants, improve retention/referral, control maintenance cost, fund selected bets |
| **Giảm dần/cắt bỏ (Dog)** | Low market growth and weak relative position | Release capacity unless a strategic role or credible repositioning case exists | Harvest, reposition, bundle, partner, freeze new enrollment, or exit with learner-protection plan |

Use **Phòng thủ/dòng tiền** only when competitive strength and full economic contribution are evidenced. Maturity or historical familiarity is insufficient.

Use **Giảm dần/cắt bỏ** as a decision category, not a command to terminate immediately. Check active learner obligations, credential commitments, refunds, contracts, data retention, staff transition, teach-out, and brand impact.

## 6. Run the workflow

1. State the portfolio decision, horizon, focal entity, and constrained resources.
2. List decision units and separate materially different segments, channels, or delivery models.
3. Build the evidence ledger and reconcile product, market, financial, delivery, and outcome definitions.
4. Pass prerequisite gates and mark each fatal failure.
5. Define both axes, formulas, thresholds, evidence window, and confidence before placing products.
6. Classify each unit and record the reason in one sentence.
7. Add the economic role and strategic role without changing the quadrant secretly.
8. Select the action: scale, defend, nurture, harvest, reposition, partner, freeze, or exit.
9. Attach owner, capacity, investment ceiling, KPI, next gate, review date, and failure action.
10. Review quarterly by default, or sooner when cash, legal, quality, partner, or market conditions change materially.

For an incomplete dataset, produce a provisional matrix plus the missing-evidence plan. Do not force false precision.

## 7. Apply decision rules

### Star

- Scale only to the verified delivery bottleneck.
- Track contribution, cash collection, completion, outcome quality, learner support load, and refund risk by cohort.
- Protect differentiation and channel control.
- Downgrade or pause scale when quality, economics, or capacity breaches the agreed gate.

### Tiềm năng cần nuôi dưỡng

- Set one falsifiable hypothesis and one decision deadline.
- Cap cash, founder attention, faculty capacity, and technical build.
- Prefer paid pilots and real conversion evidence over surveys or verbal partner interest.
- Promote to Star only after competitive strength and delivery economics are evidenced.
- Stop, partner, or reposition when evidence fails.

### Phòng thủ/dòng tiền

- Verify that the product funds its direct and allocated operating structure.
- Protect the profitable segment; do not spend broadly to reverse a structurally mature market.
- Simplify delivery, variants, channels, and support while preserving learning outcomes.
- Define the cash released for Star or Question Mark bets; do not drain the product until quality or trust collapses.
- Watch for declining competitive strength that signals harvest, repositioning, or exit.

### Giảm dần/cắt bỏ

- Compare harvest, reposition, bundle, license, partner, freeze, and exit options.
- Identify whether the product still provides a necessary entry, credential, cross-sell, partner, or learner-protection role.
- Stop new investment unless a time-bound repositioning hypothesis passes.
- Create a teach-out and obligation plan before reducing delivery or enrollment.
- Release owner and delivery capacity only after responsibilities are transferred.

### Transition logic

Define what evidence moves a unit:

- Question Mark → Star: proven advantage, repeatable acquisition, viable economics, delivery capacity, and outcomes.
- Star → Cash Cow: growth slows while competitive strength and contribution remain strong.
- Cash Cow → decline/exit: competitive strength, contribution, or relevance falls below the agreed threshold.
- Dog → Question Mark: a specific repositioning creates credible high-growth demand and passes a capped pilot.

## 8. Produce the output

Use [output-templates.md](output-templates.md), section **H. Danh mục BCG cho sản phẩm giáo dục**.

At minimum show:

| Unit | Growth evidence | Competitive evidence | BCG group | Confidence | Fatal gate | Economic/strategic role | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then show:

| Unit | Investment posture | Owner | Capacity/budget ceiling | KPI | Next gate | Review date | Failure action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Close with:

- products to scale now;
- products to defend and optimize;
- products to nurture through capped pilots;
- products to harvest, reposition, freeze, or exit;
- evidence still missing;
- the single portfolio decision leadership must approve next.
