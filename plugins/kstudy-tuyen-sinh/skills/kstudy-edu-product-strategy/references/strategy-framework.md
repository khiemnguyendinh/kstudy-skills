# Education Product Strategy Framework

Use this framework for a complete strategy or a material product decision. Adapt depth to the request; do not force every section into a narrow answer.

## 1. Strategic boundary and ecosystem thesis

Define the focal entity first. Map other components only by the value they provide and receive.

| Component | Possible role | Evidence to request |
|---|---|---|
| Education institution | Admission, teaching, assessment, credentialing | License, permitted programs, delivery capacity |
| Academy/EdTech partner | Curriculum, delivery, technology, recruitment | Faculty, LMS, outcomes, operating proposal |
| Community | Trust, leads, events, retention | Active members, conversion, engagement, commercial mechanism |
| Employer/industry partner | Practice, projects, internships, jobs | Facilities, experts, hiring demand, prior outcomes |
| Startup services | Setup, management, marketing, community | Service scope, unit economics, support capacity |
| Overseas employment partner | Language, placement, procedure, post-return pathway | License, destination demand, contracts, compliance |

Write the ecosystem thesis as one sentence:

`For [priority learner/customer], the ecosystem moves them from [starting problem] through [learning/credential] to [work/business outcome], while each component owns [distinct role].`

Do not let ecosystem breadth obscure which entity owns revenue, liability, learner experience, and outcomes.

Do not assume the focal education institution is the ecosystem center. Determine the center from:

- who owns trust and the input funnel;
- who owns the recurring relationship and community;
- who coordinates value before and after education;
- who controls the main data and economic loop.

When appropriate, separate two architectures:

| Axis | Purpose | Typical role of the institution | Economic treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core ecosystem loop | Move members from need to education, work/startup, and continued development | Skills, credentials, academic quality, assessment | Explicit transfer pricing or revenue share with the ecosystem center |
| Independent education business | Serve sectors that do not strongly reinforce the core community | Education management, assessment, credentialing, articulation | Separate product P&L supported by symbiotic partners |

Map partners by what they contribute: recruitment, curriculum, experts, facilities, equipment, projects, internships, employment, technology, or outcome data. Map the institution separately by admission, academic governance, learner records, assessment, credentials, quality, and progression.

## 2. Customer priority and jobs-to-be-done

Segment by decision context rather than demographics alone. Common segments include:

- existing business owners needing compliance, management, and growth;
- practitioners needing skills and recognized credentials;
- career switchers needing capability, portfolio, and qualification;
- first-time founders needing an end-to-end launch pathway;
- secondary students and parents choosing dual-track education;
- employers needing standardized workforce capability;
- workers seeking domestic or overseas placement.

For each segment, capture:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Job | What progress are they trying to make? |
| Trigger | Why act now? |
| Barrier | What blocks purchase, learning, or completion? |
| Proof | What makes the offer credible? |
| Outcome | What measurable result is expected? |
| Buyer | Who pays and who decides? |
| Channel | Where can the segment be reached efficiently? |

Prioritize segments using strategic fit, urgency, willingness to pay, reachable distribution, delivery capability, legal feasibility, and outcome evidence.

## 3. Product architecture

Organize offers by role, not only duration:

- **Entry product**: diagnostic, event, workshop, short certificate, lead qualification.
- **Cash engine**: proven demand and fast collection; funds current operations.
- **Core transformation**: deeper capability plus credential and outcome.
- **Progression product**: articulation to higher qualifications or advanced specialization.
- **Outcome service**: internship, employment, startup, compliance, or business growth.
- **Strategic option**: new sector, dual-track school pathway, overseas employment, or new geography.

Every product must specify target segment, promise, credential, duration, modality, price, acquisition route, delivery owner, outcome, next product, economics, legal basis, and stage.

Use three horizons:

| Horizon | Purpose | Default decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Now | Protect cash and repair delivery | Scale only what has demand and controllable quality |
| Next | Add adjacent products or partnerships | Pilot after economics and delivery readiness pass |
| Later | Create strategic options | Buy evidence cheaply before committing infrastructure |

Limit concurrent strategic bets according to actual owner capacity. A long product list is not a portfolio strategy.

For mixed portfolios, explicitly assign economic roles:

- short-cycle products may be the cash engine that keeps the organization alive;
- long-cycle formal programs, school pathways, articulation, employment, or startup services may be profit/growth engines;
- strategic options should consume only a capped evidence budget until their gates pass.

When the management decision is to allocate attention, capacity, or investment across products, apply [bcg-portfolio-model.md](bcg-portfolio-model.md) after fatal legal, cash, delivery, and outcome gates. Keep product role and BCG position separate: an entry product can be a Star, Question Mark, Cash Cow, or exit candidate depending on its market and competitive evidence.

## 4. Education product scorecard

Score each criterion from 1 to 5 only when evidence supports the score. Show unknown instead of guessing.

| Criterion | Suggested weight | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Strategic/customer fit | 15 | Priority segment and urgent job |
| Demand/distribution access | 15 | Leads, conversion, partner/channel access |
| Unit economics and cash timing | 15 | Retained revenue, variable cost, collection schedule |
| Legal and credential feasibility | 15 | Permission, program basis, approval path |
| Delivery readiness | 15 | Curriculum, faculty, facilities, LMS, assessment |
| Employment/business outcome | 10 | Employer demand, projects, placement mechanism |
| Partner quality/dependency | 5 | Due diligence and replacement risk |
| Time to launch | 5 | Critical-path evidence |
| Scalability/data readiness | 5 | SOP, capacity, source of truth |

Do not let a high total score override a fatal legal, quality, or cash-risk gate.

## 5. Unit economics and cash flow

Keep these measures distinct:

- **Gross billings** = price × paid learners.
- **Retained revenue** = gross billings − channel/partner shares − discounts/refunds, subject to accounting treatment.
- **Contribution per learner** = retained revenue − learner-level delivery and processing costs.
- **Program contribution** = contribution per learner × paid learners − program-specific fixed costs.
- **Operating result** = total program contribution − shared payroll, office, management, technology, compliance, and taxes as applicable.
- **Cash balance effect** = cash collected − cash paid in the period; account for instalments, receivables, partner holding time, refunds, and prepaid costs.
- **Break-even volume** = avoidable/fixed operating cost ÷ contribution per learner, only if definitions are consistent.

Request price, payment schedule, refund rate, scholarship/discount, partner commission, faculty cost, facilities/materials, assessment/credential cost, processing/support, marketing/sales compensation, shared overhead, tax treatment, collection timing, and current capacity.

Stress-test base, downside, and upside scenarios using user-provided assumptions. Show formulas and label estimates. Do not optimize revenue while receivables, partner-held tuition, or uncontrolled certificate issuance creates leakage.

### Multi-engine financial model

Do not aggregate economically different portfolios too early.

| Engine | Required question | Minimum management rule |
|---|---|---|
| Cash engine | Does it fund its full direct and allocated operating structure? | Reach break-even independently; do not rely on expected long-term profit |
| Profit/growth engine | What is collected, when, at what full cost and working-capital exposure? | Separate P&L, cash schedule, receivables, partner share, and stop-loss by product |
| Total institution | What remains after consistent shared-cost allocation? | Aggregate only reconciled numbers using the same definitions |

For each milestone show retained revenue/cash, operating result, cost ceiling, volume, and timing. If intermediate monthly values are interpolated between user-stated endpoints, label them **planning assumptions**, not accounting forecasts.

## 6. Go-to-market and channel architecture

Evaluate direct, partner, community, school, employer, and content/event channels separately. For each channel, define:

- lead ownership and source attribution;
- sales responsibility and service level;
- commercial share and allowable promotions;
- who collects money and issues invoices;
- learner communication responsibility;
- data access and privacy;
- quality and brand controls;
- dispute, refund, and termination rules.

Treat a community as a distribution asset only when access, event rights, lead handling, revenue share, and conversion measurement are explicit.

For tiered or network-based distribution:

- reconstruct the full price waterfall across institution, network builder, agent, and end customer;
- include internal sales commissions, sales support, promotions, documents, and processing when comparing direct versus B2B channels;
- do not assume direct enrollment is cheaper because no external partner is visible;
- treat revenue-share rates as strategic constraints when volume is sensitive to them;
- optimize process cost, error rate, support workload, and conversion before cutting partner economics;
- transfer document-completion and learner-care responsibilities to B2B partners only with explicit SLA, training, data access, and escalation rules.

## 7. Operating model before automation

Map the learner lifecycle:

`lead → qualification → counseling → application → payment → admission → class/LMS → attendance/progress → assessment → credential → employment/startup/progression → alumni/renewal`

For each state define owner, entry criteria, exit criteria, source of truth, SLA, notification, exception, approval, and audit evidence.

Sequence transformation:

`organization/RACI → capacity → policy/SOP → data model → pilot workflow → technology integration → training/UAT → controlled go-live → scale`

AI may answer approved FAQs, classify, extract, remind, summarize, and route. Keep human approval for legal eligibility, exceptional admission, financial waiver/refund, formal academic decision, assessment exception, and credential issuance.

### AI-native completion model

Treat AI-native as complete only when all six layers exist:

1. **Organization**: job descriptions, RACI, decision rights, capacity, and accountable owner.
2. **Process**: digital workflow, statuses, SLA, exception routes, and fallback.
3. **Data**: one learner ID, source of truth, permissions, audit trail, and reconciliation.
4. **AI assignment**: approved tasks, knowledge source, input/output, escalation, and human approval.
5. **Adoption**: role-based training, UAT, real usage evidence, and performance KPI.
6. **Management**: dashboards, operating cadence, incident review, and continuous improvement.

An LMS, CRM, chatbot, or automation workflow alone does not make an institution AI-native.

## 8. Outcome system

Define outcomes by cohort and measurement window. Possible metrics:

- enrollment-to-start rate;
- completion and assessment pass rate;
- credential issuance accuracy and turnaround;
- employment, income progression, business launch, or compliance outcome;
- placement within a defined period after graduation;
- employer/learner satisfaction with response bias disclosed;
- progression to higher qualification;
- renewal, referral, or cross-sell;
- cost per successful outcome, not only cost per enrollment.

Document numerator, denominator, cohort, source, owner, frequency, and confidence level for every KPI.

## 9. Roadmap logic

Build the roadmap from constraints and gates, not calendar ambition:

1. Stabilize the cash engine and remove leakage.
2. Prove the operating model on a narrow pilot.
3. Launch the nearest adjacent program with existing permission and delivery readiness.
4. Validate partner-led programs through proposals, due diligence, and short pilots.
5. Scale long-cycle or high-regulation initiatives only after outcome evidence.

For each phase, specify objective, deliverables, owner, dependency, KPI, decision date, pass threshold, and failure action.

For an executive two-year strategy, prefer these horizons:

| Horizon | Management question | Typical deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| First 15 days | Is the foundation ready? | Organization, job descriptions, RACI, resource plan, automated enrollment/records, training platform, process/data standards |
| Days 16–30 | Is the whole institution operating AI-native? | Department workflows, AI assistants, one learner record, dashboards, adoption, governance |
| Days 31–90 | Does the model scale without losing cash or quality? | Capacity, break-even, partner adoption, quality evidence, controlled pilots |
| One year | Which engines and programs have proven profitable growth? | Product P&Ls, outcomes, partner capability, cash reserve |
| Two years | What validated portfolio should scale? | Focused portfolio, repeatable operations, measurable outcomes |
| After two years | Which strategic options deserve a new plan? | Vision and options only unless evidence supports detail |

Avoid a day-by-day schedule in an executive strategy. Use daily steps only for a cutover, crisis, legal deadline, or an explicitly requested implementation checklist.
