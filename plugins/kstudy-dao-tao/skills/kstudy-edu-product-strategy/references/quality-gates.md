# Quality Gates and Strategic Challenges

Use these gates to challenge recommendations. A high portfolio score cannot override a failed fatal gate.

## Gate 0: Strategic fit

Pass when:

- the priority customer and urgent job are explicit;
- the initiative strengthens the focal entity's role in the ecosystem;
- the actual ecosystem center is identified from the value and relationship loop rather than assumed;
- core ecosystem programs and relatively independent education businesses are separated;
- it fits the agreed horizon and does not dilute the cash engine;
- the owner can state why this initiative should precede alternatives.

Failure action: reframe, defer, or remove.

## Gate 1: Legal and academic feasibility

Pass when evidence exists for:

- permitted field, level, modality, and credential;
- admission requirements and required records;
- approved curriculum, faculty, facilities, assessment, and record ownership;
- lawful recruitment, contracting, tuition collection, invoice, data processing, and credential issuance roles;
- required authority notification or approval.

Failure action: stop external claims and enrollment; obtain qualified review or change the model.

## Gate 2: Customer and demand evidence

Pass when:

- the target segment, buyer, problem, and promise are specific;
- reachable channels are identified;
- evidence goes beyond stated interest: paid pilot, qualified pipeline, conversion history, employer demand, or comparable proof;
- acquisition economics can be measured.

Failure action: run a low-cost demand test; do not build full delivery.

## Gate 3: Unit economics and cash

Pass when:

- retained revenue and contribution are defined consistently;
- delivery, sales, partner, support, assessment, credential, refund, and shared costs are visible;
- payment timing and working-capital exposure are modeled;
- downside volume does not create unacceptable cash risk;
- the business has a stop-loss threshold.
- cash and profit engines have separate P&L, cash timing, cost ceilings, and owners;
- expected profit from long-cycle products is not used to conceal a loss-making cash engine;
- interpolated targets are labeled as planning assumptions rather than forecasts.

Failure action: redesign price, channel, cost, collection, cohort size, or scope.

## Gate 4: Delivery readiness

Pass when:

- curriculum and learning outcomes are approved;
- qualified instructors and replacements exist;
- facilities, equipment, LMS, learning materials, assessment, and learner support are ready;
- capacity is quantified at each bottleneck;
- the learner lifecycle SOP and source of truth are usable;
- completion and quality risks have owners.

Failure action: pilot narrowly or delay launch.

## Gate 5: Partner readiness

Classify evidence correctly:

`idea < meeting < verbal commitment < MOU < proposal < due diligence passed < signed agreement < pilot passed < scaled delivery`

Pass when roles, deliverables, capacity, economics, data, brand, learner care, IP, quality, compliance, liability, reconciliation, termination, and replacement are documented and verified.

Failure action: request a proposal or run due diligence; do not publish a launch date based on goodwill.

## Gate 6: Operating readiness

Pass when:

- each workstream has one Accountable and at least one Responsible person;
- capacity, budget/vendor, access, and decision rights are confirmed;
- SOP, data fields, status definitions, exception routes, audit log, and fallback exist;
- staff and partners pass UAT or scenario tests;
- technology has an operator and acceptance criteria.
- every department in AI-native scope has a digital process, shared data, defined AI tasks, human approvals, adoption evidence, and a dashboard.

Failure action: hold go-live or restrict it to an internal pilot.

An LMS, CRM, chatbot, or automation workflow does not pass the AI-native gate by itself.

## Gate 7: Outcome readiness

Pass when:

- the outcome and measurement window are explicit;
- numerator, denominator, cohort, source, owner, and frequency are defined;
- employer, startup, progression, or overseas placement mechanisms exist;
- marketing claims do not exceed evidence.

Failure action: reduce the promise and build an outcome-data loop.

## Gate 8: Roadmap and artifact quality

Pass when:

- near-term phases show executive objectives, workstreams, deliverables, owners, KPIs, and failure actions;
- daily steps appear only for cutover, crisis, hard deadlines, or explicit user demand;
- one- and two-year targets are SMART, while later horizons remain vision unless evidence supports detail;
- Vietnamese is primary and avoidable English or long presentation prose is removed;
- ecosystems, flows, timelines, and financial relationships use legible diagrams when useful;
- formal names and titles are consistent with the artifact's audience;
- rendered DOCX/PDF pages have been visually checked.

Failure action: simplify, redraw, correct naming, or shorten the roadmap before approval.

## Common traps and countermeasures

| Trap | Diagnostic | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Product-list strategy | Many programs, no priority or stop list | Rank by horizon and fatal gates |
| BCG by intuition | Quadrants are assigned without explicit axes, evidence window, thresholds, or confidence | Define the strategic unit and evidence ledger; label proxy-based work BCG điều chỉnh |
| Star inflation | Revenue growth or leadership enthusiasm is treated as proof of a Star | Require market evidence, relative competitive strength, economics, delivery capacity, and outcome quality |
| Cash Cow illusion | A mature or familiar course is called defensive despite weak contribution or declining competitive strength | Verify retained cash, full cost, retention, quality, and the cost of maintaining relevance |
| Exit by quadrant | A low-growth, weak-position product is cut without considering learner obligations, legal commitments, strategic role, or repositioning value | Run harvest/reposition/partner/freeze/exit options through owners, gates, and a learner-protection plan |
| Revenue illusion | Price × enrollment treated as profit | Rebuild retained revenue, contribution, overhead, and cash timing |
| Direct-channel illusion | Direct enrollment excludes internal commission, sales support, and processing cost | Compare channels using the full price and cost waterfall |
| Commission shock | Partner share is cut to improve margin without testing volume sensitivity | Reduce operating waste first; change channel economics only with evidence and fallback |
| Volume-first scaling | Marketing grows faster than hồ sơ, teaching, or assessment capacity | Measure bottleneck capacity and pilot throughput |
| Technology-first transformation | CRM/LMS/chatbot selected before SOP and owner | Lock RACI, process, data, operator, and acceptance tests first |
| Partner optimism | MOU or verbal commitment treated as launch readiness | Use evidence ladder and due diligence |
| Ecosystem blur | Community, school, and partner revenue/liability are mixed | Define entity-level roles and transfer pricing/revenue share |
| Center-by-default | The school is drawn at the center because it owns the document | Identify the center from funnel, community, recurring relationship, data, and value loop |
| Cross-subsidy blindness | Long-term expected profit hides a loss-making short-term engine | Separate P&L and stop-loss by engine; require the cash engine to self-fund |
| Credential leakage | Informal collection, uncontrolled reissue, weak reconciliation | Official payment only, ticket, dual approval, audit, inventory reconciliation |
| Employment claim inflation | Rate based only on respondents or unclear window | Define cohort denominator and verify within a stated period |
| Owner bottleneck | Founder approves every operational exception | Create decision matrix and escalation thresholds |
| Manual shadow system | Staff revert to chat and spreadsheets | Enforce a single record ID, audit, fallback queue, and daily reconciliation |
| Tool-as-transformation | A chatbot, LMS, or CRM is described as AI-native | Require organization, process, data, AI/human boundaries, adoption, and management evidence |
| Micromanaged strategy | Executive roadmap lists daily tasks | Replace with phase objectives, workstreams, deliverables, owners, KPIs, and gates |
| Visual prose | Long text boxes and arrow characters represent a flow | Use a real diagram and keep labels short |
| Naming drift | Conversational salutation appears in formal strategy | Apply an artifact-specific title map and run a final terminology check |

## Red-team questions

Ask at least the most relevant five before finalizing a major strategy:

1. Which assumption, if false, destroys the plan?
2. Is the current cash engine profitable after the full operating structure, or only contribution-positive?
3. What fails first if enrollment doubles next month?
4. Which product should be removed to protect focus?
5. Does the institution have authority to make every advertised claim?
6. Who owns the learner when recruitment, delivery, and credentialing are split?
7. What happens if the key partner exits after enrollment?
8. What evidence proves the promised employment or startup outcome?
9. Which decision is irreversible, and can it be converted into a pilot?
10. What data or behavior would make leadership stop the initiative?
11. Which exact product-segment-channel unit is being classified, and would aggregation hide a Star or a loss-maker?
12. What evidence would move each product to another BCG quadrant at the next review?
