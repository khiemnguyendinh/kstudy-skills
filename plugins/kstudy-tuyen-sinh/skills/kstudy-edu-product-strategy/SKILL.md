# Kstudy Education Product Strategy

## Operating principles

1. Start with the business decision, not the product list or technology.
2. Identify the real ecosystem center from customer flow, trust, data, recurring value, and economic control. Do not assume the education institution is the center.
3. Treat the school, community, platform, employer network, startup services, and partners as separate components with explicit roles, revenue, liability, data, and outcomes.
4. Separate confirmed facts, assumptions to validate, estimates, and recommendations. Never fill missing numbers with invented data.
5. Separate the current cash engine from future profit engines. Require each engine to show its own revenue, cost, cash timing, and operating result.
6. Prioritize owner, capacity, legal authority, delivery ability, SOP, data, and acceptance criteria before automation or expansion.
7. Define AI-native as an operating model built on organization, digital processes, shared data, AI-assisted work, human approvals, adoption, and auditability—not as a chatbot or software purchase.
8. Build executive roadmaps by phase, objective, deliverable, owner, KPI, and gate. Do not create day-by-day task calendars unless the user explicitly requests one or a fragile cutover requires it.
9. Use Vietnamese as the primary language for Vietnamese decision-makers. Keep only widely understood terms such as AI, KPI, RACI, LMS, CRM, automation, B2B, and B2C when they improve clarity.
10. Use diagrams for ecosystems, flows, timelines, and financial relationships. Avoid text boxes containing long prose and arrow characters.
11. Preserve the naming convention of the formal artifact. Do not copy conversational salutations into institutional documents; for Phoenix strategy documents, use **Thầy Khiêm**, not **Anh Khiêm**.
12. Treat legal, licensing, diploma, certificate, tax, tuition collection, data protection, and overseas employment claims as high-stakes. Identify what needs qualified verification; do not present strategic inference as legal advice.

## Select the engagement mode

Infer the narrowest mode that satisfies the request:

- **Discovery**: clarify ambition, focal entity, customers, cash engine, evidence, capabilities, constraints, and naming convention.
- **Ecosystem and portfolio strategy**: identify the ecosystem center, supporting nodes, independent business lines, product roles, BCG position, priorities, investment posture, and exclusions.
- **Product validation**: evaluate one program, learner segment, delivery model, or outcome pathway.
- **Economics**: define retained revenue, contribution, full operating result, cash timing, break-even, and scenario targets.
- **Partnership strategy**: define roles, economics, governance, due diligence, data, liability, and stage gates.
- **Operating model and AI-native transformation**: design organization, roles, process, data, tools, approvals, adoption, and management dashboards.
- **Implementation roadmap**: translate approved choices into phased outcomes, owners, KPIs, dependencies, and go/no-go gates.
- **Strategy audit and document revision**: challenge contradictions, revise structure and language, create visuals, and validate the final artifact.

For a complete strategy, combine modes in this order:

`Discovery → ecosystem boundary → customer priority → economics → portfolio → legal/delivery readiness → partnership/GTM → operating model → roadmap → artifact QA`

## Run the strategy workflow

### 1. Frame the decision and artifact

State:

- the decision to be made;
- focal legal entity or business unit;
- ecosystem in scope and what remains outside it;
- time horizon;
- artifact and audience;
- formal names, titles, and terminology.

If one ambiguity can materially change the strategy, ask one high-leverage question before analyzing further.

### 2. Build an evidence ledger

Maintain four categories:

- **Đã xác nhận**: explicitly supplied or verified.
- **Giả định cần kiểm chứng**: necessary but unconfirmed.
- **Ước tính kế hoạch**: calculated or interpolated from stated targets.
- **Khuyến nghị**: proposed choice, threshold, sequence, or experiment.

Reconcile contradictions before using a number. Preserve both values and ask whether they differ by period, product, channel, definition, or actual versus target.

### 3. Interview adaptively

Use [references/interview-playbook.md](references/interview-playbook.md). Ask one question at a time. Prefer the question that most affects cash survival, legal feasibility, ecosystem role, delivery capacity, or an irreversible commitment. Stop discovery when enough evidence exists to make the decision.

### 4. Map the ecosystem correctly

Determine separately:

1. Which component owns trust and the input funnel?
2. Which component provides skills, credentials, community, employment, or startup support?
3. Which component retains the relationship after the learner outcome?
4. Which education lines directly reinforce the core ecosystem?
5. Which education lines are relatively independent businesses supported by symbiotic partners?

Show value provided, value received, revenue owner, liability owner, data owner, and outcome owner. If useful, draw two axes: the core ecosystem loop and independent education businesses.

### 5. Diagnose economics before growth

Keep these measures distinct:

`gross billings → retained revenue/cash → learner contribution → program contribution → operating result → cash balance effect`

When the portfolio has different economic roles, model two or more engines separately:

- **Cash engine**: fast collection and proven demand; must at least self-fund its operating structure.
- **Profit/growth engine**: longer-cycle programs, progression, employment, or other services; must not conceal losses in the cash engine.

For each engine, define baseline, monthly target, full cost ceiling, break-even volume, collection timing, receivables/refunds, owner, and stop-loss. Only aggregate after allocating shared cost consistently. Label interpolated milestones as planning assumptions, not accounting forecasts.

Compare direct and B2B channels using the full price and cost waterfall, including internal commission, sales support, promotions, and processing. When partner volume is sensitive to revenue share, optimize operating waste before changing commercial terms.

### 6. Prioritize the portfolio and design gates

Organize offers by role: entry, cash engine, core transformation, progression, outcome service, or strategic option. Limit concurrent bets according to owner capacity.

When the decision concerns portfolio allocation, use [references/bcg-portfolio-model.md](references/bcg-portfolio-model.md):

1. Define the unit as `product × segment × channel × delivery model × geography × period` at the narrowest level supported by data.
2. Pass fatal legal, academic, cash, delivery, partner, and outcome gates before using BCG to justify scale.
3. Define and evidence the two axes. Use classical market growth and relative market share when available. Otherwise label the result **BCG điều chỉnh** and disclose the proxy indicators and confidence.
4. Classify each unit as **Star**, **Tiềm năng cần nuôi dưỡng (Question Mark)**, **Phòng thủ/dòng tiền (Cash Cow)**, or **Giảm dần/cắt bỏ (Dog)**.
5. Attach an investment posture, owner, capacity, KPI, review date, next gate, and failure action. Do not assign blanket budget percentages or make an exit decision from the quadrant alone.

Treat economics, cash timing, learning outcomes, legal feasibility, delivery quality, strategic role, and dependency risk as overlays or gates, not hidden substitutes for the two BCG axes. A fast-growing product is not automatically a Star; a mature product is not automatically a Cash Cow.

Convert uncertain initiatives into staged commitments:

`concept → proposal/evidence → due diligence → short pilot → review → formal launch → scale`

Attach evidence, owner, deadline, minimum threshold, approval authority, and failure action to every gate. Treat verbal commitment and MOU as interest, not delivery readiness.

### 7. Design the operating model and AI-native transformation

Sequence transformation:

`organization and roles → capacity → process and SOP → data model → AI assignment and human approvals → pilot → training/UAT → controlled go-live → measurement → scale`

For each core lifecycle state define owner, input, status, SLA, output, exception, source of truth, approval, audit evidence, and fallback.

Define each AI assistant by role, input, output, knowledge source, permissions, memory/context, escalation rule, human approval, and KPI. Keep humans responsible for legal eligibility, financial exceptions, academic decisions, assessment exceptions, and credential issuance.

### 8. Build an executive roadmap

Use the shortest horizon that supports the decision. For a two-year strategy, default to:

- **First 15 days**: organization, job descriptions, RACI, resource plan, automated enrollment/records, training and assessment platform, process and data standardization.
- **Days 16–30**: AI-native transformation across departments, one learner record, AI assistants, dashboards, staff adoption, and governance.
- **Days 31–90**: prove capacity, quality, cash balance, partner adoption, and controlled adjacent pilots.
- **One year**: prove profitable growth, priority programs, partner capability, and outcomes.
- **Two years**: scale only validated engines and programs.
- **After two years**: state vision and strategic options; do not fabricate detailed plans without evidence.

For every phase specify objective, workstreams, deliverables, owner, KPI, decision gate, and failure action. Use daily steps only for a cutover plan, crisis response, or when explicitly requested.

### 9. Produce and validate the artifact

Use [references/document-standards.md](references/document-standards.md).

- Lead with decisions, economics, and priority actions.
- Prefer short sentences, keywords, tables, and real diagrams.
- Use Vietnamese equivalents instead of unnecessary English.
- Check titles, names, dates, units, formulas, duplicated targets, and ecosystem roles.
- For DOCX/PDF, inspect rendered pages and verify that tables and diagrams remain legible.
- If a requested visual API fails, disclose the failure and use a clearly labeled fallback; never claim the API generated the fallback.

### 10. Close with decisions

End each substantial iteration with:

- decisions confirmed;
- assumptions or evidence still missing;
- immediate actions and owners;
- stop conditions;
- the single next question or approval needed.

## Quality bar

Reject or revise an answer that:

- merely reorganizes the product list;
- applies BCG without defining the strategic unit, axes, evidence window, thresholds, and confidence;
- calls a product Star from revenue growth alone, calls a mature product Cash Cow without proving competitive strength and cash contribution, or recommends cutting a product from its quadrant alone;
- assumes the school is the ecosystem center without mapping the value loop;
- mixes a sibling community's roles or revenue with the education entity;
- treats enrollment or tuition as profit without full cost and collection timing;
- lets long-term expected profit conceal a loss-making short-term cash engine;
- recommends technology before owner, SOP, data, operator, permissions, and acceptance criteria;
- calls a chatbot, LMS, or automation tool an AI-native transformation by itself;
- treats partner enthusiasm as operational commitment;
- uses unverified legal claims, market rankings, employment rates, or capacity figures;
- gives a roadmap with daily micromanagement instead of executive outcomes and gates;
- gives a multi-year plan with false precision beyond the available evidence;
- writes a Vietnamese strategy using avoidable English or long presentation sentences;
- represents a flow as prose plus arrows when a diagram would be clearer;
- uses the wrong formal title or copies the chat salutation into the document;
- optimizes acquisition while completion, delivery quality, assessment, or credential control is unresolved.

## Expected answer style

Use the minimum structure needed. For a full strategy, follow:

`Mục tiêu → KPI → lựa chọn chiến lược → hành động ưu tiên → mốc thời gian → người chủ trì → cổng quyết định → rủi ro/giả định`

Use tables for portfolio, economics, RACI, partners, gates, and roadmaps. Use diagrams only where relationships, flows, or timelines become materially clearer. Avoid generic market commentary unless it changes a decision.

For a BCG portfolio decision, show at minimum:

`đơn vị phân tích → bằng chứng hai trục → vị trí BCG và độ tin cậy → vai trò kinh tế/chiến lược → quyết định đầu tư → owner/KPI/gate → điều kiện chuyển nhóm hoặc dừng`
